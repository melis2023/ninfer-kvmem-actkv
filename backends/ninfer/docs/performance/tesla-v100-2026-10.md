# Tesla V100 measurements, October 2026

What a `70` build ([Tesla V100](../build-linux.md#tesla-v100-sm_70)) does on one Tesla
V100-SXM2-16GB: which models fit, the engine's prompt and decode rates per model and KV format,
the server's rates with speculation, and llama.cpp on the same card. The original setup is
[below](#how-these-were-measured). The later follow-up has a separate setup and scope; it does not
requalify the 16 GB capacity limits.

MTP pays on the V100 up to two requests at once, DFlash2 at one. From four, the batched verify

## Known limits

- With `--vision` the Volta build plans 988 MiB of workspace for a 16,384-token image (other
  targets: under 827 MiB). Its wide projections stage FP16 activations and FP32 product planes,
  8,192 patches at a time.
- Over the same image the CPU vision encoder and the device one put the first answer token's two
  likeliest choices 0.27 apart in log probability on IQ3_S. The check that compares them allows
  0.25.
- On IQ3_S, an image prompt with MTP and prefix reuse allowed (nothing reused) began its answer
  with another token than the same request without reuse (760 against 27775), so the stop on the
  second token of the latter never came (`ninfer_qwen3_5_prefix_real_test`). The cause is not
  established.
- The real-artifact test `hybrid_prefix_real` needs more than 16 GB (3.3 GB of runtime capacity
  where 2.2 GB remain), and `moe_real` and `dflash_real` need another artifact; they do not run on
  this card.

## Against the RTX 3090

The same artifacts and protocol on the RTX 3090 of the [reference page](reference-2026-09.md)
(Ternary Bonsai 2 at 390 W) and of the [IQ3_S page](../gguf.md#measurements) (420 W), decode
tok/s:

| model, KV, speculation | request | V100 | RTX 3090 | V100 / 3090 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bonsai 2, `rk4v4`, none | short chat | 73.2 | 93.8 | 0.78 |
| Bonsai 2, `rk4v4`, none | after 32,768 | 67.2 | 83.7 | 0.80 |
| Bonsai 2, `rk4v4`, none | after 131,072 | 54.6 | 64.4 | 0.85 |
| Bonsai 2, `rk4v4`, MTP 3 | short chat | 123.1 | 173.4 | 0.71 |
| Bonsai 2, `rk4v4`, MTP 3 | after 131,072 | 60.0 | 96.5 | 0.62 |
| Bonsai 2, `rk4v4`, DFlash2 5 | short chat | 116.1 | 200.8 | 0.58 |
| Bonsai 2, `rk4v4`, DFlash2 5 | after 131,072 | 45.0 | 97.5 | 0.46 |
| Bonsai 2, `rk8v4`, none | eight requests at once | 283.5 | 546.4 | 0.52 |
| Bonsai 2, `rk8v4`, MTP 3 | eight requests at once | 204.9 | 550.8 | 0.37 |
| Bonsai 2, `rk8v4`, DFlash2 5 | four requests at once | 116.4 | 334.1 | 0.35 |
| IQ3_S, `rk8v4`, none | short chat | 45.5 | 59.9 | 0.76 |
| IQ3_S, `rk8v4`, MTP 3 | short chat | 77.0 | 108.7 | 0.71 |

Prompt time of Bonsai 2 is 2.6-2.7x the RTX 3090's at 1,024 and 8,192 tokens and 3.4x at 131,072.

The two cards read memory at about the same rate (900 against 936 GB/s), so one request's
decode stays within 0.75-0.85 of the RTX 3090. The rest of the gap has four causes:

- **Prompts.** Volta multiplies integers only on its CUDA cores. Wide products therefore
  dequantize the weights to FP16 for cuBLAS, about 92 TFLOPS, where the RTX 3090 runs its integer
  tensor cores. Prompt attention past 32K tokens widens the gap further.
- **Verify rounds and batches.** Several columns cost more compute per weight byte. The T2 integer
  route covers up to eight columns. Wider rounds and batches run FP16 tensor-core products, which
  spend most of their instructions widening codes.
- **Decode attention at depth.** The Volta kernel reaches about 40% of the card's bandwidth: one
  layer's partial kernel takes 191 us at 61,440 `rk4v4` keys, against about 80 us for the bytes
  it reads (`ninfer_causal_softmax_attention_bench`, cold cache).
- **IQ3_S's grid quantizations** (IQ3_S, IQ3_XXS, IQ2_S) are bound by decoding, not by reading.
  A 5,120 x 17,408 matrix takes about 70 us in any of them: 485-550 GB/s, against 704-785 GB/s
  for its IQ4_XS and Q4_K matrices.

## How these were measured

- **Card.** One Tesla V100-SXM2-16GB at its 300 W limit (80 SMs, 1,530 MHz, HBM2 at 877 MHz; a
  streaming read reaches 856 GB/s), in a container on a host with a Xeon E5-2683 v4 (64 threads)
  and 76 GB of RAM, Ubuntu 24.04, driver 580.178.04. A Tesla P4 in the same host was not used.
- **Build.** `-DCMAKE_CUDA_ARCHITECTURES=70`, CUDA 12.9.86, GCC 13.3, Release. The
  device route profile is the built-in one.
- **llama.cpp.** `f2cc728` (2026-10-11), CUDA 12.9 for `sm_70`, `llama-bench -ngl 99 -fa 1 -r 2`,
  the GGUFs the artifacts were converted from.
- **Engine rates.** `ninfer_bench --max-ctx 4096` (17,408 for the 16K rows); one warm-up run.
- **Server.** `ninfer-serve <artifact> --max-context W --kv-capacity W --max-concurrency 1
  --gdn-state-fp16` with the KV format and speculation of the row, everything else at its default;
  [`tools/bench/refbench.py`](../../tools/bench/refbench.py) as on the
  [reference page](reference-2026-09.md#how-these-were-measured): greedy, thinking off,
  non-streaming; short chat is the mean decode rate of five 512-token answers, a depth row a cold
  document of that many tokens (PG-19, WikiText and this repository's text) and an answer of about
  400 words. Several requests at once: `--max-concurrency 8 --max-context 16384 --kv-capacity
  131072`, `rk8v4`, the short-chat prompts in turn.
- **Largest context.** `--max-context` = `--kv-capacity` bisected on whether the server starts.


## Follow-up on the 32 GB and 16 GB cards, 15-16 October

The Op and serving tests below used a V100-SXM2-32GB at 300 W, CUDA 12.8.93 and an sm_70 build.
They cover a selected attention implementation; the last paragraph checks the whole model on a
16 GB card.

### NVFP4 attention

The accepted change is limited to B=1, NVFP4 KV and TokenTile4. It uses the scalar form for
H16 with at most two key tiles, unroll2 for append or more than 256 active CTAs, and the existing
unroll8 in the remaining cached range. The decision uses actual positions, not a captured
maximum-context envelope. Other selected B=1 instantiations and the restored batched T4 code
were checked for instruction equivalence to their retained baselines.

The main twelve Op cases reduced latency by 0.995% to 6.299% against current code. Across all
152 cases, 31 paired medians increased, with a worst increase of 1.456%. H16 cached 9K and 24K
increased by 0.935% and 0.495%. One K8V4 sample increased by 15.385%; its companion samples
were unchanged, and the cause was not established. Relative to the older implementation,
H16 cached 8K and H24 cached 61K still increased by 0.847% and 0.155%. The historical
16 GB regression is not fully resolved.

Forty Engine requests and 24 HTTP outputs passed. Twelve current/selected output pairs were
identical. Engine decode throughput changed by -0.083% to +1.583%, with three of ten cases
slower; prefill changed by -0.284% to +0.107%, with four of ten slower. Twelve plain/MTP output
differences were already present in the baseline. These observations do not establish a
universal whole-model speedup. Workspace and Graph plans were unchanged in the measured
matrix. Stack allocation changed by route, and the final executables grew by about 1.02 MB.

Independent FP64 qualification, depth boundaries and final serving checks passed without
relaxing tolerances. Global unroll2 and e8 variants were rejected: they slowed 26 of 30 cases,
with worst changes around 42% and 40%.

On a V100-SXM2-16GB at 300 W (CUDA 12.8, one request), Ternary Bonsai 2 27B with an NVFP4 KV cache
ran `ninfer_bench -pg <depth>,128` in two interleaved rounds, baseline and change in alternating
order. MTP3 decode, whose T=4 verification takes the changed route, rose 1.0% at both depths: 116.68
to 117.85 tok/s after 8,192 prompt tokens and 96.05 to 97.03 after 24,576. Plain decode changed by
-0.06% and +0.04%. Prefill changed by -0.01% to -0.39%; the worst single round was -0.86% at 8,192
tokens with MTP3, inside its baseline's spread of 4.6 tok/s. The quantized-depth, NVFP4 and FP8
prompt oracle tests passed on this card.
